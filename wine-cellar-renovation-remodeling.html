Wine Cellar Renovation and Remodeling Ann Arbor MI

There are two kinds of cellar renovation. One is a room that works and looks dated. The other is a room that looks fine and does not work. They cost very different amounts.

  • A section of existing envelope opened and inspected before pricing
  • Cooling capacity rechecked against the room as it will be, not as it was
  • Existing racking assessed for reuse rather than automatically discarded
  • Cause of any previous failure identified in writing before rebuild

Cellars built in the 1990s and 2000s commonly share the same weaknesses: fibreglass batts with a poorly sealed poly barrier, a self-contained cooling unit venting into a finished space, a hollow-core door and lighting that adds heat. Each of those is individually survivable and collectively they produce a room that runs constantly and never quite holds.

The usual approach

New racking and a fresh coat of finish installed over the same failing envelope, with the same problems arriving again in three years and the new millwork now in the way of fixing them.

How we do it

The envelope opened, the cause of failure identified, and the rebuild sequenced so the invisible work is corrected before anything visible is installed.

What actually matters in a cellar renovation

Renovation is the only cellar work where the most valuable thing we do is sometimes telling you the job is bigger than you hoped.

  • Musty smell in an existing cellar almost always indicates moisture in the wall assembly rather than in the air, and no amount of ventilation or air treatment resolves it.
  • A cooling unit that runs continuously is not necessarily undersized. More often the envelope is leaking, and replacing the unit alone simply produces a larger unit running continuously.
  • Existing racking is frequently worth keeping. Redwood and cedar age well and can be dismantled, cleaned and reinstalled, which saves a meaningful part of a renovation budget.
  • Anything installed over a compromised envelope has to come off again when the envelope is eventually addressed, so sequencing the invisible work first is genuinely cheaper rather than merely correct.

Cosmetic renovation is priced like any millwork job and is reasonably predictable. Renovation following an envelope failure is priced in phases, because what we find behind the first wall determines the rest.

We quote the exploratory phase as a fixed price of its own, then produce a firm number for the remainder once we can see what we are dealing with. That avoids either of us guessing.

Before we renovate your cellar

Renovation preparation is mostly about telling us the history honestly, including the parts that are annoying to admit.

  • Tell us what the room has actually been doing. How often the unit runs, whether the temperature has drifted, whether anything smells, and how long it has been like that all narrow the diagnosis considerably.
  • Empty the cellar completely and relocate the wine to conditioned storage before we start. Renovation work is dusty and the room will not hold temperature during it.
  • Dig out any documentation from the original build if it exists. Knowing what insulation was used, and by whom, saves exploratory opening.
  • Photograph the racking before dismantling if you want it reused. Reinstalling a mixed rack layout is much faster from a photograph than from memory.

How your wine cellar renovation & remodeling project works

  1. Open and diagnose

    We remove a section of finish and inspect the envelope, framing and vapour barrier before quoting the renovation itself.

  2. Report the cause

    You get a written explanation of what failed and why, along with a clear split between cosmetic work and corrective work.

  3. Correct the envelope

    Any failed insulation, vapour barrier, door or cooling issue is rectified before new finishes go anywhere near it.

  4. Rebuild and reload

    Racking is reinstalled or replaced, the renovated cellar is run at temperature and logged, and the collection goes back in.

Wine Cellar Renovation & Remodeling questions we get asked

My cellar smells musty. Can that be fixed?

Usually yes, but not by treating the air. A musty smell in a cellar nearly always means moisture is sitting in the wall assembly, and the fix is opening it up, drying it out and correcting the vapour barrier.

Air purifiers, dehumidifiers and ozone treatments address the symptom and leave the cause in place.

Can you reuse my existing racking?

Often, yes. Solid timber racking in redwood, cedar or hardwood usually survives careful dismantling, and reusing it saves a substantial part of the budget.

Particle board and veneered racking rarely survives removal, and anything showing mould at the joints should be replaced rather than cleaned.

Do I need to be present while you renovate my cellar?

Not throughout, but the diagnostic stage is worth attending. Seeing what is behind the wall for yourself makes the subsequent decisions much easier.

We will always photograph what we find regardless.

Where should the wine go during the work?

Somewhere held between 50 and 65 degrees and away from vibration and light. A rented reefer, a friend's cellar or a commercial storage unit all work.

What does not work is a garage or an unconditioned basement corner, since a Michigan summer will put those well above the damage threshold.

Is it cheaper to renovate or start again?

It depends almost entirely on what the envelope is doing. A sound envelope with dated finishes is far cheaper to renovate. A failed envelope means stripping back to structure, at which point the saving over a new build is small.

We will tell you which of those you have before you commit to either.
